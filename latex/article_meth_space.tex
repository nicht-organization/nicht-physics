\section{Introduction \& Subject-Space Duality}
\label{sec:intro}
Classical mathematical epistemology frequently models spatial manifolds under constructive $1$-logic across both the observing agent and the underlying spatial domain ($1 \to 1 \to 1$), implicitly assuming formal verification to be an intrinsic, ontological property of space \cite{russell1903}. Within the framework of \textit{Nicht-Theorie}, we suspend this projection to analyze the operator relations separating discrete observational apertures ($1$-consistency) from invariant spatial baselines ($0$-consistency) \cite{nicht_theory_monograph, nicht_physics_paper_1}.

\subsection{Ontological Spheres: Atemporal Baselines vs.\ Dynamic \texorpdfstring{$1$-Apertures}{1-Apertures}}
To rigorously decouple spatial background dynamics from bounded observational operations without prejudging the measure-theoretic nature of space, we formalize three domain spheres \cite{nicht_theory_monograph, nicht_physics_paper_1}:
\begin{itemize}
    \item \textbf{$0$-Consistent Baselines ($B_0$):} Non-asserted theoretical states, historical state invariants, and unconditioned potential exist independently of temporal evolution \cite{nicht_theory_monograph, nicht_physics_paper_1}. Because they exert zero assertion pressure ($P_A = 0$), they require no computational verification overhead and induce zero operational latency ($\mathcal{W} = 0$) \cite{nicht_theory_monograph}.
    \item \textbf{$1$-Consistent Paradoxical Spheres:} Local observational domains and continuous physical trajectories act as localized, non-equilibrium apertures \cite{nicht_physics_paper_1}. In these domains, observation manifests as dynamic state updates—inducing a continuous negation of $0$ that generates observational noise ($\sigma$) and non-zero operational latency ($\mathcal{W} > 0$) \cite{nicht_theory_monograph, nicht_physics_paper_1}. Within the evaluation window of an active aperture, consistency is enforced via axiomatic constraints, whereas structural limits continuously introduce contextual inconsistencies.
    \item \textbf{Mathematical Space (Open Hypothesis):} We formulate the systemic consistency 
    mode of space ($0$- vs.\ $1$-consistency) as an open hypothesis, proved via \textit{reductio} in Section~\ref{sec:reductio}.    
\end{itemize}
