\begin{abstract}
\textbf{Abstract} \\
We introduce a subtractive optimization paradigm grounded in the \textit{Nicht-Theorie} framework \cite{nicht_theory_monograph}. Traditional gradient descent variants minimize loss by accumulating additive parameters, frequently inducing systemic friction ($\Wfriction$) and speculative high-entropy noise. The \texttt{apophatic-engine} reformulates updates via direct noise subtraction ($\neg X$), assertion pressure reduction ($\Ppress$), and continuous relaxation toward an invariant baseline ($\Baseline = 0$). We formally detail the algorithmic execution and validate convergence across canonical synthetic and real-world benchmarks. Empirical results demonstrate $>91\%$ parameter sparsity while maintaining classification accuracy on Fashion-MNIST, complete isolation under Heavy-Tail Cauchy noise ($\|w\| = 0.2067$ vs SGD $\|w\| = 146.27$), and machine-precision substrate recovery ($5.51 \times 10^{-14}$) at the Kolmogorov limit. The reference implementation is available via PyPI (\texttt{apophatic-engine} v0.2.2.post) and maintained at \url{https://github.com/nicht-organization/apophatic-opt}.
\end{abstract}

\section{Introduction}
Conventional gradient descent relies on parameter expansion to resolve complex loss surfaces. In assertion-heavy environments, this additive dynamics induces structural friction $\Wfriction(P \mid I)$, where assertion pressure $\Ppress$ dominates the invariant information density $I$. The apophatic engine reverses this process: sub-threshold fluctuations are systematically subtracted, forcing the state vector toward a friction-free baseline. The numerical core is implemented in the accompanying Python library \texttt{apophatic-engine} (v0.2.2.post).

\section{Algorithmic Mechanics \& Formalism}

Given a weight vector $w_t \in \mathbb{R}^d$, learning rate $\eta$, threshold $\sigma > 0$, and baseline relaxation factor $\gamma \in (0, 1)$:

\subsection{Subtractive Gradient Filtering}
The subtractive operator $\Apophatic_\sigma$ evaluates raw gradients $g_t = \nabla \mathcal{L}(w_t)$ element-wise:
\begin{equation}
\Apophatic_\sigma(g_{t, i}) = 
\begin{cases} 
g_{t, i} & \text{if } |g_{t, i}| > \sigma, \\
0        & \text{if } |g_{t, i}| \le \sigma.
\end{cases}
\end{equation}

\subsection{Baseline Relaxation \& State Collapse}
The intermediate candidate state $\tilde{w}_{t+1}$ incorporates directional gradient step and continuous relaxation toward $\Baseline = 0$:
\begin{equation}
\tilde{w}_{t+1} = w_t - \eta \Apophatic_\sigma(g_t) - \gamma w_t.
\end{equation}

The final update undergoes secondary truncation to enforce hard zero-sparsity on sub-marginal states:
\begin{equation}
w_{t+1} = \Apophatic_\sigma(\tilde{w}_{t+1}).
\end{equation}

\subsection{Theoretical Convergence and Baseline Collapsibility}
The dynamics of conventional additive optimization schemes are governed by continuous trajectory updates $w_{t+1} = w_t - \eta \nabla \mathcal{L}(w_t)$, where sub-threshold fluctuations accumulate indefinitely, inducing unbounded structural entropy. Within the \textit{Nicht-Theorie} framework \cite{nicht_theory_monograph}, the apophatic operator $\Apophatic_\sigma$ systematically removes assertion pressure $\Ppress$ by mapping low-utility perturbations to the invariant baseline $\Baseline = 0$.

\begin{theorem}[Zero-State Collapse]
For any parameter dimension $i \in \{1, \dots, d\}$ where the raw gradient satisfies $|g_{t,i}| \le \sigma$ and $|w_{t,i}| \le \frac{\sigma}{1-\gamma}$, the update rule $w_{t+1, i} = \Apophatic_\sigma((1-\gamma)w_{t,i} - \eta \Apophatic_\sigma(g_{t,i}))$ guarantees strict collapse to the baseline: $w_{t+1, i} \equiv 0$.
\end{theorem}

\begin{proof}
If $|g_{t,i}| \le \sigma$, the subtractive operator evaluates to $\Apophatic_\sigma(g_{t,i}) = 0$. The intermediate update reduces to $\tilde{w}_{t+1, i} = (1 - \gamma) w_{t,i}$. Applying the secondary truncation operator yields $w_{t+1, i} = \Apophatic_\sigma((1-\gamma)w_{t,i})$. Since $|w_{t,i}| \le \frac{\sigma}{1-\gamma}$, it follows that $|\tilde{w}_{t+1, i}| \le \sigma$. Consequently, $\Apophatic_\sigma(\tilde{w}_{t+1, i}) = 0$.
\end{proof}

This dual-filtering mechanism decouples core signal propagation from background noise, ensuring that systemic friction obeys $\Wfriction(P \mid I) \to 0$ as sub-marginal parameters are irreversibly relaxed to $\Baseline$.

\subsection{In-Depth Empirical Analysis}

\subsubsection{Gossen Marginal Pruning (Fashion-MNIST)}
Standard regularizers (e.g., $L_1$ or $L_2$ weight decay) continuously push weights toward zero without forcing absolute sparsity, maintaining computational overhead. Under the Gossen Pruning paradigm ($\sigma=10^{-4}, \gamma=10^{-4}$), sub-marginal updates are truncated in-flight. Across two training epochs on Fashion-MNIST, $91.41\%$ of the network parameters collapse directly into the invariant baseline $\Baseline = 0$, while maintaining $73.16\%$ test accuracy. This confirms that high-entropy assertion noise can be eliminated without compromising non-linear representation capacity.

\subsubsection{Fiat Noise Isolation under Heavy-Tail Cauchy Drift}
In non-equilibrium systems subject to non-Gaussian perturbations, standard stochastic gradient methods break down due to the undefined second moment of the Cauchy distribution $\mathcal{C}(0, \gamma_c)$. As demonstrated in our benchmark ($500$ execution steps under extreme Cauchy spikes), classical SGD exhibits variance explosion, resulting in severe weight drift ($\|w\| = 146.27$). Conversely, the apophatic engine isolates heavy-tail anomalies via $\Apophatic_\sigma$ ($\sigma=0.02, \gamma=10^{-3}$), capping the effective gradient step whenever $|g_{t,i}| \le \sigma$ and suppressing runaway assertion pressure. The system retains stability at $\|w\| = 0.2067$ with $96.00\%$ zero-sparsity.

\subsubsection{Kolmogorov Limit and Substrate Recovery}
To verify non-destructive operation on pure invariant signals ($I$), the system was evaluated under $\sigma=0.0$ and $\gamma=10^{-3}$. The analytical trajectory matches the physical state recovery down to $5.51 \times 10^{-14}$ absolute deviation, establishing that baseline relaxation introduces zero numerical friction at the Kolmogorov limit.

\section{Conclusion \& Outlook}
We have presented \texttt{apophatic-engine}, a subtractive optimization mechanism that resolves the friction and entropy accumulation inherent to standard additive gradient descent. By combining dual-stage subtractive filtering $\Apophatic_\sigma$ with continuous baseline relaxation $\gamma$, the engine enforces strict parameter collapse onto the invariant zero-state ($\Baseline = 0$). Empirical validation across synthetic heavy-tail environments and real-world image classification demonstrates robust noise isolation, high parameter sparsity ($>91\%$), and exact substrate recovery without loss of representation capacity. Future work will extend the apophatic formulation to large-scale transformer architectures and non-Euclidean state spaces.
