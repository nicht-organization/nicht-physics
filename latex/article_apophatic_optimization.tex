\begin{abstract}
We introduce a subtractive optimization paradigm grounded in the \textit{Nicht-Theorie} framework \cite{nicht_theory_monograph}. Traditional gradient descent variants minimize loss by accumulating additive parameters, frequently inducing systemic friction ($\Wfriction$) and speculative high-entropy noise. The \texttt{apophatic-opt} engine reformulates updates via direct noise subtraction ($\neg X$), assertion pressure reduction ($\Ppress$), and continuous relaxation toward an invariant baseline ($\Baseline = 0$). We formally detail the algorithmic execution and validate convergence across four canonical benchmarks: Jevons' rebound cancellation, Gossen marginal pruning, fiat noise isolation, and Kolmogorov friction limits ($\Wfriction \to 0$).
\end{abstract}

\section{Introduction}
Conventional gradient descent relies on parameter expansion to resolve complex loss surfaces. In assertion-heavy environments, this additive dynamics induces structural friction $\Wfriction(P \mid I)$, where assertion pressure $\Ppress$ dominates the invariant information density $I$. The apophatic engine reverses this process: sub-threshold fluctuations are systematically subtracted, forcing the state vector toward a friction-free baseline.

\section{Algorithmic Mechanics \& Formalism}

Given a weight vector $w_t \in \mathbb{R}^d$, learning rate $\eta$, threshold $\sigma > 0$, and baseline relaxation factor $\gamma \in (0, 1)$:

\subsection{Subtractive Gradient Filtering}
The subtractive operator $\Apophatic_\sigma$ evaluates raw gradients $g_t = \nabla \mathcal{L}(w_t)$ element-wise:
\begin{equation}
\Apophatic_\sigma(g_{t, i}) = 
\begin{cases} 
g_{t, i} & \text{if } |g_{t, i}| > \sigma, \\
0        & \text{if } |g_{t, i}| \le \sigma.
\end{cases}
\end{equation}

\subsection{Baseline Relaxation \& State Collapse}
The intermediate candidate state $\tilde{w}_{t+1}$ incorporates directional gradient step and continuous relaxation toward $\Baseline = 0$:
\begin{equation}
\tilde{w}_{t+1} = w_t - \eta \Apophatic_\sigma(g_t) - \gamma w_t.
\end{equation}

The final update undergoes secondary truncation to enforce hard zero-sparsity on sub-marginal states:
\begin{equation}
w_{t+1} = \Apophatic_\sigma(\tilde{w}_{t+1}).
\end{equation}

\section{Benchmark Suite \& Experimental Validation}

The numerical framework is empirically evaluated against a suite of four canonical benchmarks. \Cref{tab:benchmarks} consolidates the experimental setups, input distributions, operational parameters ($\sigma, \gamma$), and target convergence bounds:

\begin{itemize}
    \item \textbf{Jevons Rebound Cancellation}: Input gradients $g \sim \mathcal{N}(2, 0.5)$ under $\sigma=0.5, \gamma=0.01$. Asserts containment of capacity inflation, bounding systemic weight growth ($\|w\| \le C$).
    \item \textbf{Gossen Marginal Pruning}: Input decay $g_i \to 0^+$ under $\sigma=0.1, \gamma=0.05$. Suppresses sub-marginal weight branches where updates yield efficiency below systemic friction, forcing rapid collapse to absolute zero ($\Baseline$).
    \item \textbf{Fiat Noise Isolation}: Isotropic uniform noise $g \sim \mathcal{U}(-0.08, 0.08)$ under $\sigma=0.1, \gamma=0.00$. Guarantees complete suppression of pure entropy noise without directional accumulation ($g_{\text{eff}} = 0$).
    \item \textbf{Kolmogorov Friction Limits}: Pure signal under $\sigma=0.0, \gamma=0.00$. Formally verifies identity mapping and exact substrate recovery under noise-free conditions, proving zero friction ($\Wfriction = 0$).
\end{itemize}
