% --- ENGLISH ABSTRACT ---
\begin{abstract}
Building upon the epistemological framework of Subject-Space Duality and
$0$-consistency established in Paper~0~\cite{nicht_metaphysics_paper_0}, we
critically reassess the Apophatic Inversion Space $A_0 \equiv
\widehat{\mathbb{C}} \cong S^2$ as a purported resolution of the Riemann
Hypothesis~\cite{riemann1859ueber}. We demonstrate that the previously
introduced analytical friction metric $\mathcal{W}(z) = 4\cdot
(\operatorname{Re}(z))^2$ is a tautological construct: its zero locus coincides
with the critical line by definition, but this does not constrain the location
of non-trivial zeros of the Riemann zeta function $\zeta(s)$. To expose this
deficiency, we construct a concrete \emph{non-Riemann space} using the
Davenport--Heilbronn function, which satisfies a functional equation analogous
to $\zeta(s)$ yet violates the Riemann Hypothesis. In that space, the very
same metric $\mathcal{W}(z)$ again vanishes on the shifted critical line
$\operatorname{Re}(z)=0$, while off-axis zeros persist. Consequently, the
proposed invariant fails as a proof of RH. We provide numerical evidence
illustrating the insufficiency and discuss the broader epistemological
implications of testing RH-dependent arguments against deliberately constructed
non-Riemannian models.
\end{abstract}

\section{Introduction \& Relation to Paper 0}
Building upon the epistemological framework of Subject-Space Duality and
$0$-consistency established in Paper~0~\cite{nicht_metaphysics_paper_0}, we
revisit the Apophatic Inversion Space $A_0 := \widehat{\mathbb{C}} =
\mathbb{C} \cup \{\infty\} \cong S^2$ as a proposed observation domain for the
Riemann Hypothesis~\cite{riemann1859ueber}. The original formulation claimed
that the critical line $\operatorname{Re}(s)=1/2$ is an immediate topological
consequence of zero-friction inversion closedness. Here we argue that this
conclusion rests on a tautology and does not advance our understanding of the
Riemann zeta function.

To substantiate this criticism, we employ the concept of a \emph{non-Riemann
space}: a mathematical model in which the analogue of the Riemann Hypothesis
fails, yet the same formal machinery can be applied. The existence of such a
space demonstrates that the proposed metric $\mathcal{W}(z)$ is insensitive to
the actual location of zeros and hence cannot serve as a proof.

\section{The Inversion-Closed Riemann Space \texorpdfstring{$\mathcal{R}_{\text{RH}}$}{R\_RH}}

\subsection{Coordinate Shift and Inversion Operator}
Let $s \in \mathbb{C}$ be the classical complex variable in the critical
strip $0 < \operatorname{Re}(s) < 1$. We define the centered coordinate shift:
\begin{equation}
z := s - \frac{1}{2} \implies s = \frac{1}{2} + z .
\end{equation}
Under this transformation, the critical line $\operatorname{Re}(s) = 1/2$
maps directly onto the imaginary axis:
\begin{equation}
\mathcal{B}_0 := \{ z \in \mathcal{R}_{\text{RH}} \mid \operatorname{Re}(z) = 0 \}.
\end{equation}
The functional equation $\xi(s) = \xi(1-s)$ corresponds in
$\mathcal{R}_{\text{RH}}$ to the point-inversion operator $I_{\text{RH}}:
\mathcal{R}_{\text{RH}} \to \mathcal{R}_{\text{RH}}$:
\begin{equation}
I_{\text{RH}}(z) := -z \quad \text{with} \quad I_{\text{RH}}^2 = \text{id}.
\end{equation}

\subsection{Analytical Friction Metric \texorpdfstring{$\mathcal{W}(z)$}{W(z)}}
The friction operator $\mathcal{W}(z)$ is defined as the squared distance under
dual reflection:
\begin{equation}
\mathcal{W}(z) := \left| z - I_{\text{RH}}(z) \right|^2 = |z - (-z)|^2 = 4 \cdot (\operatorname{Re}(z))^2 .
\end{equation}
Properties of $\mathcal{W}(z)$:
\begin{enumerate}
    \item \textbf{Non-negativity:} $\mathcal{W}(z) \ge 0$ for all $z \in
    \mathcal{R}_{\text{RH}}$.
    \item \textbf{Zero-Friction Invariant:} $\mathcal{W}(z) = 0 \iff
    \operatorname{Re}(z) = 0 \iff \operatorname{Re}(s) = 1/2$.
    \item \textbf{Off-Axis Noise:} Any hypothetical off-axis zero
    ($\operatorname{Re}(z) = \delta \neq 0$) produces strictly positive friction
    $\mathcal{W}(z) = 4\delta^2 > 0$.
\end{enumerate}

\noindent\textbf{Critical Observation.} Property~2 is tautological: the set
where $\mathcal{W}(z)=0$ is \emph{defined} to be exactly the shifted critical
line. It does not assert that $\zeta(s)$ has no zeros with
$\operatorname{Re}(z)\neq 0$; it merely restates the condition
$\operatorname{Re}(s)=1/2$ in different notation. To see this more sharply, we
now introduce a non-Riemannian model.

\section{The Non-Riemann Counterexample: Davenport--Heilbronn Space}

Consider the Davenport--Heilbronn function $F(s)$, a Dirichlet series
satisfying a functional equation of the same shape as the Riemann zeta
function, but for which the analogue of the Riemann Hypothesis is known to be
false. Concretely, $F(s)$ is a linear combination of Dirichlet $L$-functions
which admits a meromorphic continuation and satisfies
\[
F(s) = \chi(s) F(1-s)
\]
for an appropriate factor $\chi(s)$; yet it possesses non-trivial zeros with
$\operatorname{Re}(s) \neq 1/2$ (see, e.g., the classical construction by
Davenport and Heilbronn). In the shifted coordinate $z = s - 1/2$, the
functional equation again induces the involution $z \mapsto -z$, and we may
define the \emph{same} friction metric
\[
\mathcal{W}_{F}(z) = 4 \cdot (\operatorname{Re}(z))^2 .
\]
By construction, $\mathcal{W}_{F}(z) = 0$ exactly on the line
$\operatorname{Re}(z)=0$, i.e., $\operatorname{Re}(s)=1/2$. However, $F(s)$ has
zeros off this line, corresponding to points with $\operatorname{Re}(z) \neq 0$.
Thus, in the Davenport--Heilbronn space, the zero-friction condition is
satisfied trivially on the critical line, but fails to exclude off-axis zeros.
This demonstrates that the metric $\mathcal{W}$ is unable to distinguish
between Riemannian and non-Riemannian settings; it is not a genuine detector of
zero locations.

\section{Stochastic \& Numerical Validation}
To illustrate the tautological character of the friction metric, we conduct a
numerical experiment that compares the Riemann zeta function $\zeta(s)$ and the
Davenport--Heilbronn function $F(s)$. We generate a set of sample points
$s = \sigma + it$ with $\sigma \in (0,1)$, $t \in [10, 1000]$, and evaluate
\[
\mathcal{W}(s) = 4 \left(\sigma - \frac12\right)^2 .
\]

the real part. Therefore, for both $\zeta$ and $F$, the values of

The identical values confirm that $\mathcal{W}$ cannot distinguish between the

\section{Conclusion}
The Apophatic Inversion Space and its associated friction metric
$\mathcal{W}(z)$ do not resolve the Riemann Hypothesis. The condition
$\mathcal{W}(z)=0$ is equivalent to $\operatorname{Re}(s)=1/2$ by definition
and hence carries no information about the location of zeros. The existence of
the Davenport--Heilbronn function, which shares the same formal symmetries but
violates RH, underscores the vacuity of the proposed invariant. We conclude
that any purported proof of RH must engage with the analytic properties of
$\zeta(s)$ that go beyond mere symmetry; otherwise it is prone to the same
tautological trap. The non-Riemann space serves as a powerful test: if a
proposed argument also ``proves'' the analogue of RH for Davenport--Heilbronn,
then it is almost certainly flawed.
